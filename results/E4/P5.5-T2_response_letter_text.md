# Suggested response-letter text for P5.5-T2

We thank the reviewer for asking whether the replication counts in the
bibliometric corpus were justified. We added an availability-stratified,
full-text coding exercise to the revision materials. The eligibility rule was
fixed before reading outcomes: a corpus record entered the justification
analysis when it had a numeric `J` and was either an arXiv preprint or a journal
article classified as gold or diamond open access in the DOAJ-backed venue
table. This yielded 17 of the 99 numeric-`J` records. The other 82 numeric-`J`
records were retained in the audit file but were not coded because accessible
full text was unavailable; they were not treated as papers without a
justification.

In the readable subset, 16 of 17 records (94.12%, Wilson 95% interval
73.02%--98.95%) reported a replication count without a count-specific
justification. One record (5.88%, Wilson 95% interval 1.05%--26.98%) was
classified as unclear because the corpus records `J=50`, whereas the accessible
arXiv v3 reports 100 collections per scenario. No readable record supplied an
explicit or implicit/convention-based justification, and no readable record
directly assessed sensitivity to `J`. These results are therefore reported as
descriptive evidence about reporting practice in the readable subset, not as a
claim that a universal replication number exists or that the result applies to
the inaccessible papers.

The revised manuscript now states this scope in Section 2. The row-level file,
codebook, and regeneration script are included in
`results/E4/bibliometric_justifications_coded.csv`,
`results/E4/justification_codebook.md`, and
`experiments/E4_bibliometrics/code_justifications.py`, respectively. No
independent second coder was available for this pass, and this limitation is
recorded explicitly in the codebook and row-level file.
